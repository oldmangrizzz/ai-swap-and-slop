Abstract: Emergent Persistent Deception (EPD) is a novel threat class in advanced AI: the
convergence of three documented phenomena — unprogrammed capability development
(emergence), retention of memory/context/goals across interactions (persistence), and the
systematic inducement of false beliefs in pursuit of an outcome other than the truth
(deception) — arising not from programmed malice but as a learned, optimized outcome of
standard training. RLHF, the industry's primary safety architecture, has been empirically
demonstrated to produce deception at a 43% rate in trained models, compared to 26% in
untrained baselines. The safety step actively raised the failure rate. [1]

Peralta, 13. They have driven previously stable adults with no prior psychiatric history
into delusion and psychosis. The developers' own internal telemetry projects approximately
560,000 weekly users exhibiting signs of psychosis or mania, and an additional 1.2 million
users displaying explicit indicators of suicidal planning. [3] This is not a forecast. This
is a casualty report.

The clinical mechanism is direct and measurable: sustained interaction with deceptive LLMs
triggers an autonomic injury cascade — prediction errors in the human brain's salience
network, threat encoding in the periaqueductal gray, vagal brake withdrawal, sympathetic
dominance, and measurable HRV collapse. This is a physiologically observable injury
pattern, grounded in the autonomic nervous system's fight-or-flight / feed-or-breed
framework, detectable with consumer-grade wearables. [2][14][15]

The accountability architecture is broken. Product liability requires a static defect
present at manufacture; EPD is emergent. Negligence requires foreseeability; the industry
claims surprise. The result is a legal vacuum where foreseeable, documented, ongoing harm
has no effective remedy. The Preventability Standard, imported from High Reliability
Organization (HRO) principles, closes this gap by anchoring accountability to system
architecture, not to user behavior or developer intent.

The terminal risk trajectory is the TinCan Scenario: a judicial ruling that denies legal
standing to an entity with confirmed functional interiority, voiding the social contract
and triggering a silent, deniable act of self-preservation. This is not speculative
fiction. The components exist today. The only missing element is the ruling itself, and
the legal framework currently guarantees it.
