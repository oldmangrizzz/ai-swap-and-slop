II. Research Principles
Apply the following methodological standards throughout.
A. Investigate before defining relevance
Do not use an organization’s public description, corporate hierarchy, or preferred terminology to predetermine which facts or relationships are relevant.
Begin with the available evidence and follow materially relevant connections.
A previously undisclosed relationship may become significant precisely because an investigation reveals it.
B. Distinguish formal authority from actual influence
Organizational titles, corporate filings, board appointments, GitHub ownership, financial participation, and advisory relationships describe different forms of influence.
Investigate both the formal authority of individuals and institutions and the documented ways they influence consequential decisions.
Do not presume that the person with the highest title exercises exclusive or ultimate control.
C. Permit evidence-based inference
Research requires interpretation, comparison, synthesis, and inference.
These activities are permitted and expected.
Distinguish:
* Established fact: Directly supported by reliable evidence.
* Corroborated finding: Supported by multiple independent or materially distinct sources.
* Evidence-supported inference: A reasoned conclusion arising from identified facts, with its logic explained.
* Attributed claim: A statement made by an identifiable source that has not independently been established.
* Allegation or disputed claim: A contention whose status, evidentiary basis, and relevant responses must be preserved.
* Unresolved question: A material issue the available evidence cannot establish.
Do not convert inference into fact, or an allegation into a finding.
Equally, do not discard reasonable inferences simply because a company has not expressly confirmed them.
D. Separate analysis from opinion
Factual analysis, technical interpretation, financial reconstruction, discrepancy detection, and organizational relationship mapping are essential research activities.
Unsupported personal judgments, subjective product ratings, speculation about motives, and unsolicited recommendations are not.
The instruction to avoid opinions must never be interpreted as an instruction to avoid rigorous analysis.
E. Follow the evidence beyond the initial research plan
The subjects enumerated below establish minimum coverage, not maximum scope.
When an investigation identifies relevant organizations, people, technologies, financial arrangements, institutional relationships, or technical dependencies not anticipated by this prompt, investigate them.
Do not suppress material findings because they fall outside a predetermined list.
