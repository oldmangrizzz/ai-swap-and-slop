## What the scientific results mean

### 2020: proof of some pupil-based task-demand signal
Stolte, Gollan and Ansorge, 'Tracking visual search demands and memory load through pupil dilation,' *Journal of Vision*, 20(6):21, DOI 10.1167/jov.20.6.21, open at PubMed Central. Two controlled experiments involved visual working memory and visual search. The online modeling approach recovered meaningful modeled pupil responses and classified manipulated conditions above chance. It does not show that eye metrics uniquely identify any person's health state outside laboratory tasks. Original eye-tracking setup used a Pupil Labs camera plus a world camera and controlled lighting/task conditions.

### 2023: randomized pediatric VR educational study, not eye biomarker validation
Lietz et al., 'Dose-response of virtual reality training of paediatric emergencies in a randomised simulation-based setting,' *Acta Paediatrica*, DOI 10.1111/apa.16847. Thirty-six medical students randomized to monthly, a single repeat at three months, or no additional training. The simulator, developed by SOMA, portrayed a six-month-old infant with hypovolemic shock requiring resuscitation. Monthly practice improved task performance against less-frequent practice. This supports SOMA's experience creating functional clinical simulation software; the participants used Oculus Quest 2 and an action-scored VR scenario, not a validated LUCHS cognitive-health sensing device.

Other primary evidence includes Medical University of Vienna's 2022 annual report describing SOMA as an XR training partner, a 2023 neonatal educational editorial disclosing Michael Wagner's role on SOMA's advisory board, and a public shoulder-dystocia VR study protocol naming SOMA's iMED platform. These establish collaboration but no diagnostic performance of consumer eyewear.

### 2024: a published null result matters
Gugerell, Gollan, Stolte, Ansorge, 'Studying the Role of Visuospatial Attention in the Multi-Attribute Task Battery II,' *Applied Sciences* 14(8):3158, DOI 10.3390/app14083158. In this specific simulated aviation-like multicomponent workload experiment, the authors did not detect expected links between their studied attentional tests or pupil-derived cognitive-load indices and overall MATB-II task performance. Do not generalize this to all measurement approaches or all contexts; do treat it as evidence that cognitive state sensing and real-world performance prediction are different validation problems. The authors reported an ethics protocol, grant number 880102 and a public data repository (DOI 10.6084/m9.figshare.25540156).

### 2025: Conscious Perception Index under a narrow task
Gollan and Raggam, 'Beyond Gaze: Quantifying Conscious Perception Through an Innovative Eye Tracking Biomarker,' *Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction* 9(3), DOI 10.1145/3725831, presented ETRA 2025. The conference abstract reports 83.3% logistic-regression classification accuracy distinguishing conscious interaction from observation in a controlled VR change-blindness study. This does not indicate Alzheimer's, dementia, mental illness or cognitive-health prediction accuracy. Full ACM paper was not available for complete methods audit through the tested sources.

### 2025: aviation emergency-landings paper
Gollan and colleagues, 'Analysis of Visual Behavior and Cognitive Load of Pilots during Engine Failures with an Emergency Landing Assistance System in Mixed Reality,' PETRA 2025, DOI 10.1145/3733155.3733197. This supports active aviation-performance research lineage, not LUCHS health validation. Full paper was not obtained in the open search.

### 2026: surgical training paper
Gollan, Holub, Raggam and Hruby, 'From Gaze to Competency: A Multidimensional Eye-Tracking-Based Model of Skill Development in Surgical Simulator Training,' HCII 2026, Springer, DOI 10.1007/978-3-032-29548-4_14. Five medical trainees completed da Vinci simulator tasks with eye tracking. The paper reports a 25.8% average increase in a derived composite competency score combining gaze indicators and direct task metrics across sessions. Authors emphasize this is exploratory and not a definitive validation of skill grading. Sample size is five, and the study is not clinical assessment of future cognitive decline.

### 2026: attention-classifier conference paper
Kallmayer, Raggam, Gollan, 'Internal vs. External Attention: Context-Agnostic Classification from Gaze Kinematics,' ETRA 2026, DOI 10.1145/3797246.3805838, three pages. The official indexed proceedings and DBLP confirm authorship and publication. A full, independent accuracy audit was not possible from the accessible abstract/metadata alone.

### July 2026: Hogrefe/SOMA longitudinal cognitive health pilot
SOMA/Hogrefe technical write-up 'Towards Cognitive Health Analytics based on Eye Tracking', July 8, 2026 (not independently peer-reviewed journal publication). Twenty-nine volunteers across four weekly lab visits, four hours of mobile eye tracking at each, eight cognitive testing timepoints overall. Tasks: Wisconsin Card Sorting, Counting Span, d2-R concentration. Eye features: saccadic targeting precision and ambient-versus-focal exploration; methods say they need no outward-scene video. Hierarchical three-factor model separates fluctuations within a person from mean differences between people. Within-person targeting precision associated with better cognitive flexibility and ambient exploration with working memory (reported 89% credible intervals excluding zero). Between-person intervals all included zero; selective-attention link remained uncertain. d2-R had a practice effect. The model's fit to its included cognitive test outcomes should NOT be relabeled 'accuracy of eye tracking to predict cognition' without an eye-only external test. No published independent clinical sensitivity/specificity or prospective decline predictions were found.

## Privacy: the high-consequence issue

The LUCHS site promises user ownership, opt-in citizen-science sharing and no outward-facing camera. The published website privacy policy chiefly addresses website visitor and contact-form data, including email and IP address, plus statutory GDPR rights. It does not disclose a complete device biometric data flow, exact retention, local vs remote inference, raw-eye-image processing, encryption, access control, sharing to processors, anonymization method or withdrawal behavior. This is a disclosure gap, NOT evidence data are currently being mishandled.

The risk is not theoretical. Independently published peer-reviewed research documents that gaze trajectories can carry re-identifiable behavioral information, despite removal of explicit names. A 2023 IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics paper studied privacy and re-identification trade-offs in eye tracking, DOI 10.1109/TVCG.2023.3247048. A 2026 article 'Through the looking glass: eye tracking biometrics and the loss of anonymity in extended reality', DOI 10.1007/s10207-026-01231-3, reports 96.61% user identification accuracy **in a specific VR/video dataset and classifier**, not on LUCHS data. This is why 'anonymized' should be accompanied by specific technical guarantees, re-identification assessment and access limitations. Health data and biometric data used for identification can receive special protection under GDPR Article 9. Legal classification depends on purpose and facts.

## Regulatory position

No LUCHS-specific FDA clearance, EU MDR certification or clinical indication was verified in the public material examined. This is not a definitive claim that none exists. The distinction between general wellness and diagnosing/predicting/preventing a disease matters: the FDA's January 2026 General Wellness guidance treats low-risk lifestyle claims differently from disease-oriented claims. LUCHS's 'predicting and preventing cognitive decline' language is an aspirational research statement in a marketing context, not evidence of demonstrated clinical effectiveness. Formal intended-use labeling and manufacturer regulatory documentation should settle what the company actually claims it can detect.

## What the scientific results mean

### 2020: proof of some pupil-based task-demand signal
Stolte, Gollan and Ansorge, 'Tracking visual search demands and memory load through pupil dilation,' *Journal of Vision*, 20(6):21, DOI 10.1167/jov.20.6.21, open at PubMed Central. Two controlled experiments involved visual working memory and visual search. The online modeling approach recovered meaningful modeled pupil responses and classified manipulated conditions above chance. It does not show that eye metrics uniquely identify any person's health state outside laboratory tasks. Original eye-tracking setup used a Pupil Labs camera plus a world camera and controlled lighting/task conditions.

### 2023: randomized pediatric VR educational study, not eye biomarker validation
Lietz et al., 'Dose-response of virtual reality training of paediatric emergencies in a randomised simulation-based setting,' *Acta Paediatrica*, DOI 10.1111/apa.16847. Thirty-six medical students randomized to monthly, a single repeat at three months, or no additional training. The simulator, developed by SOMA, portrayed a six-month-old infant with hypovolemic shock requiring resuscitation. Monthly practice improved task performance against less-frequent practice. This supports SOMA's experience creating functional clinical simulation software; the participants used Oculus Quest 2 and an action-scored VR scenario, not a validated LUCHS cognitive-health sensing device.

Other primary evidence includes Medical University of Vienna's 2022 annual report describing SOMA as an XR training partner, a 2023 neonatal educational editorial disclosing Michael Wagner's role on SOMA's advisory board, and a public shoulder-dystocia VR study protocol naming SOMA's iMED platform. These establish collaboration but no diagnostic performance of consumer eyewear.

## Privacy: the high-consequence issue

The LUCHS site promises user ownership, opt-in citizen-science sharing and no outward-facing camera. The published website privacy policy chiefly addresses website visitor and contact-form data, including email and IP address, plus statutory GDPR rights. It does not disclose a complete device biometric data flow, exact retention, local vs remote inference, raw-eye-image processing, encryption, access control, sharing to processors, anonymization method or withdrawal behavior. This is a disclosure gap, NOT evidence data are currently being mishandled.

The risk is not theoretical. Independently published peer-reviewed research documents that gaze trajectories can carry re-identifiable behavioral information, despite removal of explicit names. A 2023 IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics paper studied privacy and re-identification trade-offs in eye tracking, DOI 10.1109/TVCG.2023.3247048. A 2026 article 'Through the looking glass: eye tracking biometrics and the loss of anonymity in extended reality', DOI 10.1007/s10207-026-01231-3, reports 96.61% user identification accuracy **in a specific VR/video dataset and classifier**, not on LUCHS data. This is why 'anonymized' should be accompanied by specific technical guarantees, re-identification assessment and access limitations. Health data and biometric data used for identification can receive special protection under GDPR Article 9. Legal classification depends on purpose and facts.

## Regulatory position

No LUCHS-specific FDA clearance, EU MDR certification or clinical indication was verified in the public material examined. This is not a definitive claim that none exists. The distinction between general wellness and diagnosing/predicting/preventing a disease matters: the FDA's January 2026 General Wellness guidance treats low-risk lifestyle claims differently from disease-oriented claims. LUCHS's 'predicting and preventing cognitive decline' language is an aspirational research statement in a marketing context, not evidence of demonstrated clinical effectiveness. ### The clinical research connection is real—but specific

One notable receipt is a 2023 randomized study in *Acta Paediatrica* involving **36 medical students** completing a virtual pediatric emergency involving an infant in hypovolemic shock.

SOMA helped develop the VR scenario. The study investigated how repeated simulation training affected performance, with better results following more frequent practice. The educational intervention and outcomes were real; **the trial did not establish that SOMA's eye-tracking algorithms could evaluate or diagnose cognitive health**. <Cite ref="turn335973view0"/>

That distinction also applies to other advertised institutional associations. A recognizable logo, a training software collaboration or a hardware partnership is not equivalent to an independent clinical-validation report.

## 6. Privacy: removing the outward camera does not remove the sensitive data

LUCHS's privacy positioning is deliberate: no outward-facing recording camera, user-controlled data and optional participation in its citizen-science research program.

But the relevant information is still extraordinarily personal. Continuous eye tracking can potentially reveal behavioral patterns, and independently published work has demonstrated the capacity to identify people from gaze features under particular experimental conditions.

For example, a 2026 eye-tracking biometrics study reported **96.61% user-identification accuracy in its specific VR dataset and model**. That figure is *not* a measured identification rate for LUCHS, but it demonstrates why removing names from a gaze dataset cannot automatically establish anonymity. <Cite ref="turn753461search14"/>

I reviewed LUCHS's public-facing privacy material. It discusses ordinary website and contact-form data but does not provide a complete production-device explanation of raw eye-image handling, derived biometric records, on-device versus cloud inference, retention, deletion, research-recipient access or independent anonymization testing. <Cite ref="turn574105view0"/>

So there are two separate propositions:

**Established:** The company says participation in its research-sharing program will be opt-in.

**Unresolved:** Whether the eventual collection, inference, retention and anonymization architecture has been independently tested and whether it delivers the privacy properties claimed.

There is no basis from the reviewed material to assert improper data sharing or covert surveillance.

## 7. The medical and regulatory boundary

The public marketing describes insights into cognitive load, attention, fatigue and performance. Its citizen-science language also gestures toward predicting and preventing cognitive decline.

Those are different levels of claim.

An indicator of performance during a particular task is not the same as a validated screening instrument for neurological impairment. A future disease-prediction claim would require appropriately designed clinical evidence and a regulatory assessment tied to the manufacturer's precise intended use.

I did not verify a LUCHS-specific FDA clearance or EU medical-device certification in the accessible public record. Nor could I establish the final intended-use labeling. That uncertainty matters more than simply labeling the glasses a medical device or a wellness product in advance. The FDA's January 2026 General Wellness guidance explicitly distinguishes low-risk wellness functions from disease-oriented medical uses. <Cite ref="turn395815search0"/>

## 8. The documents I would demand from SOMA

These are the highest-value questions remaining after the public-source investigation:

1. **Independent validation:** A blinded external study performed with actual LUCHS hardware, including participant demographics, exclusions, accuracy, failure rates and prespecified outcomes.
2. **Algorithm documentation:** Definitions, units, calibration rules, uncertainty estimates and reference standards for all five advertised cognitive measures.
3. **Clinical claims:** Evidence for any proposed prediction, screening or prevention of cognitive decline, including appropriate sensitivity, specificity and prospective follow-up.
4. **Hardware architecture:** Production camera/sensor arrangement, sampling frequency, battery performance, processor, connectivity, prescription-lens accommodation and low-light performance.
5. **Illumination compensation:** An engineering explanation of how the no-outward-camera design replaces the luminance information described in the patent.
6. **Data governance:** A diagram showing raw images and derived signals at every storage and processing boundary, with encryption, retention, deletion and access permissions.
7. **Citizen-science safeguards:** Study protocols, consent documents, research partners, actual anonymization methods and independent re-identification risk assessment.
8. **Regulatory documentation:** Formal intended use, EU and U.S. classification strategy, and the claims that will appear on the product at launch.

Those are not speculative allegations. They are requests for measurable, inspectable evidence directly connected to the company's public promises.

ELLIPSIZATION ...]ights. It does not disclose a complete device biometric data flow, exact retention, local vs remote inference, raw-eye-image processing, encryption, access control, sharing to processors, anonymization method or withdrawal behavior. This is a disclosure gap, NOT evidence data are currently being mishandled.

The risk is not theoretical. Independently published peer-reviewed research documents that gaze trajectories can carry re-identifiable behavioral information, despite removal of explicit names. A 2023 IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics paper studied privacy and re-identification trade-offs in eye tracking, DOI 10.1109/TVCG.2023.3247048. This is why 'anonymized' should be accompanied by specific technical guarantees, re-identification assessment and access limitations. Health data and biometric data used for identification can receive special protection under GDPR Article 9. Legal classification depends on purpose and facts.

## Regulatory position

No LUCHS-specific FDA clearance, EU MDR certification or clinical indication was verified in the public material examined. This is not a definitive claim that none exists. The distinction between general wellness and diagnosing/predicting/preventing a disease matters: the FDA's January 2026 General Wellness guidance treats low-risk lifestyle claims differently from disease-oriented claims. LUCHS's 'predicting and preventing cognitive decline' language is an aspirational research statement in a marketing context, not evidence of demonstrated clinical effectiveness. Formal intended-use labeling and manufacturer regulatory documentation should settle what the company actually claims it can detect.

## What the scientific results mean

### 2020: proof of some pupil-based task-demand signal
Stolte, Gollan and Ansorge, 'Tracking visual search demands and memory load through pupil dilation,' *Journal of Vision*, 20(6):21, DOI 10.1167/jov.20.6.21, open at PubMed Central. Two controlled experiments involved visual working memory and visual search. The online modeling approach recovered meaningful modeled pupil responses and classified manipulated conditions above chance. It does not show that eye metrics uniquely identify any person's health state outside laboratory tasks. Original eye-tracking setup used a Pupil Labs camera plus a world camera and controlled lighting/task conditions.

### 2023: randomized pediatric VR educational study, not eye biomarker validation
Lietz et al., 'Dose-response of virtual reality training of paediatric emergencies in a randomised simulation-based setting,' *Acta Paediatrica*, DOI 10.1111/apa.16847. Thirty-six medical students randomized to monthly, a single repeat at three months, or no additional training. The simulator, developed by SOMA, portrayed a six-month-old infant with hypovolemic shock requiring resuscitation. Monthly practice improved task performance against less-frequent practice. This supports SOMA's experience creating functional clinical simulation software; the participants used Oculus Quest 2 and an action-scored VR scenario, not a validated LUCHS cognitive-health sensing device.

Other primary evidence includes Medical University of Vienna's 2022 annual report describing SOMA as an XR training partner, a 2023 neonatal educational editorial disclosing Michael Wagner's role on SOMA's advisory board, and a public shoulder-dystocia VR study protocol naming SOMA's iMED platform. These establish collaboration but no diagnostic performance of consumer eyewear.

### 2024: a published null result matters
Gugerell, Gollan, Stolte, Ansorge, 'Studying the Role of Visuospatial Attention in the Multi-Attribute Task Battery II,' *Applied Sciences* 14(8):3158, DOI 10.3390/app14083158. In this specific simulated aviation-like multicomponent workload experiment, the authors did not detect expected links between their studied attentional tests or pupil-derived cognitive-load indices and overall MATB-II task performance. Do not generalize this to all measurement approaches or all contexts; do treat it as evidence that cognitive state sensing and real-world performance prediction are different validation problems. The authors reported an ethics protocol, grant number 880102 and a public data repository (DOI 10.6084/m9.figshare.25540156).

### 2025: Conscious Perception Index under a narrow task
Gollan and Raggam, 'Beyond Gaze: Quantifying Conscious Perception Through an Innovative Eye Tracking Biomarker,' *Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction* 9(3), DOI 10.1145/3725831, presented ETRA 2025. The conference abstract reports 83.3% logistic-regression classification accuracy distinguishing conscious interaction from observation in a controlled VR change-blindness study. This does not indicate Alzheimer's, dementia, mental illness or cognitive-health prediction accuracy. Full ACM paper was not available for complete methods audit through the tested sources.

### 2025: aviation emergency-landings paper
Gollan and colleagues, 'Analysis of Visual Behavior and Cognitive Load of Pilots during Engine Failures with an Emergency Landing Assistance System in Mixed Reality,' PETRA 2025, DOI 10.1145/3733155.3733197. This supports active aviation-performance research lineage, not LUCHS health validation. Full paper was not obtained in the open search.

### 2026: surgical training paper
Gollan, Holub, Raggam and Hruby, 'From Gaze to Competency: A Multidimensional Eye-Tracking-Based Model of Skill Development in Surgical Simulator Training,' HCII 2026, Springer, DOI 10.1007/978-3-032-29548-4_14. Five medical trainees completed da Vinci simulator tasks with eye tracking. The paper reports a 25.8% average increase in a derived composite competency score combining gaze indicators and direct task metrics across sessions. Authors emphasize this is exploratory and not a definitive validation of skill grading. Sample size is five, and the study is not clinical assessment of future cognitive decline.

### 2026: attention-classifier conference paper
Kallmayer, Raggam, Gollan, 'Internal vs. External Attention: Context-Agnostic Classification from Gaze Kinematics,' ETRA 2026, DOI 10.1145/3797246.3805838, three pages. The official indexed proceedings and DBLP confirm authorship and publication. A full, independent accuracy audit was not possible from the accessible abstract/metadata alone.

### July 2026: Hogrefe/SOMA longitudinal cognitive health pilot
SOMA/Hogrefe technical write-up 'Towards Cognitive Health Analytics based on Eye Tracking', July 8, 2026 (not independently peer-reviewed journal publication). Twenty-nine volunteers across four weekly lab visits, four hours of mobile eye tracking at each, eight cognitive testing timepoints overall. Tasks: Wisconsin Card Sorting, Counting Span, d2-R concentration. Eye features: saccadic targeting precision and ambient-versus-focal exploration; methods say they need no outward-scene video. Hierarchical three-factor model separates fluctuations within a person from mean differences between people. Within-person targeting precision associated with better cognitive flexibility and ambient exploration with working memory (reported 89% credible intervals excluding zero). Between-person intervals all included zero; selective-attention link remained uncertain. d2-R had a practice effect. The model's fit to its included cognitive test outcomes should NOT be relabeled 'accuracy of eye tracking to predict cognition' without an eye-only external test. No published independent clinical sensitivity/specificity or prospective decline predictions were found.

## Privacy: the high-consequence issue

The LUCHS site promises user ownership, opt-in citizen-science sharing and no outward-facing camera. The published website privacy policy chiefly addresses website visitor and contact-form data, including email and IP address, plus statutory GDPR rights. It does not disclose a complete device biometric data flow, exact retention, local vs remote inference, raw-eye-image processing, encryption, access control, sharing to processors, anonymization method or withdrawal behavior. This is a disclosure gap, NOT evidence data are currently being mishandled.

The risk is not theoretical. Independently published peer-reviewed research documents that gaze trajectories can carry re-identifiable behavioral information, despite removal of explicit names. A 2023 IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics paper studied privacy and re-identification trade-offs in eye tracking, DOI 10.1109/TVCG.2023.3247048. A 2026 article 'Through the looking glass: eye tracking biometrics and the loss of anonymity in extended reality', DOI 10.1007/s10207-026-01231-3, reports 96.61% user identification accuracy **in a specific VR/video dataset and classifier**, not on LUCHS data. This is why 'anonymized' should be accompanied by specific technical guarantees, re-identification assessment and access limitations. Health data and biometric data used for identification can receive special protection under GDPR Article 9. Legal classification depends on purpose and facts.

## Regulatory position

No LUCHS-specific FDA clearance, EU MDR certification or clinical indication was verified in the public material examined. This is not a definitive claim that none exists. The distinction between general wellness and diagnosing/predicting/preventing a disease matters: the FDA's January 2026 General Wellness guidance treats low-risk lifestyle claims differently from disease-oriented claims. LUCHS's 'predicting and preventing cognitive decline' language is an aspirational research statement in a marketing context, not evidence of demonstrated clinical effectiveness. Formal intended-use labeling and manufacturer regulatory documentation should settle what the company actually claims it can detect.

# 02 · What the Instrument Can See

::: {.eyebrow}
SIGNALS / ALGORITHMS / INFERENCE
:::

A person looks at a page. Their eyes fixate, jump, blink, and adjust to light. A camera and an algorithm can quantify aspects of that behavior. In some experimental settings, those numbers covary with aspects of attention or mental effort.

The instrument observes **pupil dimensions, gaze position, fixations, saccades and related eye-movement features**. It does not directly observe a thought. It does not receive an unambiguous label from the brain saying, *I am tired now*, or *my working memory has declined*. Those are interpretive outputs of a model trained and evaluated under particular conditions. [R03](#receipt-03) [R16](#receipt-16)

SOMA describes five analytical concepts in its broader technology portfolio:

| Company label | What the model attempts to infer |
|:--|:--|
| Cognitive Load | Task-related mental effort, using pupil dynamics and compensation for light reflexes |
| Conscious Perception Index | Engagement with externally perceived information versus other attention states |
| Attention Type Classifier | Pattern or type of attentional allocation |
| Cognitive Fatigue | Variation in estimated mental exhaustion or endurance |
| Cognitive Performance | A combined indicator derived from multiple constituent signals |

LUCHS marketing presents four consumer-facing dashboard concepts: **attention, cognitive load, cognitive fatigue and cognitive performance**. That is not proof all five B2B modules are deployed identically, or that their outputs are equally validated. [R01](#receipt-01) [R03](#receipt-03) [R13](#receipt-13)

The crucial problem is **confounding**. A pupil responds to illumination, autonomic state and multiple physiological influences. Gaze changes with the task, the display, the environment and the person. A statistical model can compensate for some of this. But a compensation method must be tested under the conditions in which it will be used.

Picture the same wearer reading at a desk, stepping into bright sunlight, speaking with a friend and driving home at dusk. The observable signals and the cognitive interpretation change together. If the model is wrong about why a pupil changed, a beautifully designed dashboard merely displays the mistake in a better font.

This is not a dismissal of eye tracking. It is a requirement that the claim be as specific as the evidence.

::: {.finding}
**ANALYSIS**  
Optical measurement validity, algorithmic construct validity, performance prediction and clinical usefulness are separate tests. A success in one is not an automatic pass in the next.

# 04 · Twenty-Nine People, Eight Test Points

::: {.eyebrow}
HOGREFE / COGNITIVE HEALTH PILOT
:::

The most relevant precursor to the consumer cognitive-health story came from a 2026 collaboration involving **SOMA, Hogrefe, and the Medical University of Vienna**.

The reported pilot enrolled **29 people**. Over **four weeks**, participants attended weekly laboratory sessions, wore mobile eye trackers for roughly four hours per visit, and undertook ordinary activities including reading, conversation and games. Investigators administered established cognitive tests at the beginning and end of sessions: **eight test points per person** in the study plan. [R26](#receipt-26)

The domains included **cognitive flexibility, working memory, and selective attention**. The eye features included measures of gaze-targeting precision and the balance between more exploratory and more focal visual scanning.

The analysis found some associations **within the same person over time**. When a person's gaze targeting was unusually precise relative to their own typical level, cognitive-flexibility scores tended to be higher; more exploratory gaze was associated with working-memory performance. The reported Bayesian intervals for these selected within-person relationships excluded zero at the study's chosen 89% level. [R26](#receipt-26)

The limits arrive immediately behind the interesting part. The selected **between-person associations were not consistently supported**. The relationship with selective attention remained uncertain. The concentration test showed a practice effect, which complicates interpreting repeated measurements. And the study did not establish how accurately an **eye-only**, externally validated model would predict an individual person's future cognitive deterioration.

A test administered eight times is not eight independent people. A model fit to several outcomes is not the same as an out-of-sample prediction test. Neither is a disease-screening sensitivity or specificity estimate.

There was a reported **12-week follow-up** under way. A follow-up in progress is not a completed result. [R26](#receipt-26)

The pilot is useful because it gives concrete hypotheses and a longitudinal direction. It is insufficient because a real-world medical claim needs enough participants, enough time, representative populations, prospective outcomes, and a prespecified standard that can actually be failed.

::: {.finding}
**HOLD FOR FURTHER EVIDENCE**  
The signal deserves investigation. A four-week exploratory pilot cannot carry the weight of a claim to predict or prevent cognitive decline.
:::

Pilot training and ordinary living do not share the same cognitive labels, lighting, motions or outcomes. A commercial product should therefore supply fresh validation of transfer rather than just inherit the prestige of a research paper.

# 06 · Privacy Does Not End at the Frame

::: {.eyebrow}
BIOMETRICS / RE-IDENTIFICATION / RESEARCH CONSENT
:::

LUCHS makes a clear marketing choice: no outward-facing camera. That reduces one class of concern about recording bystanders. It does not dissolve the separate question of what continuous observations of the **wearer's eyes** can reveal.

The company's public material promises user-controlled data and optional citizen-science participation. It proposes that volunteers may share described anonymized information with research partners in exchange for personal benchmarking and scientific participation. Those are **company statements**. [R01](#receipt-01)

The published website privacy notice chiefly addresses online browsing and contact-form information. In the reviewed record it does not provide a complete production-device data-flow diagram, processing-location policy, raw eye-image retention schedule, encryption/key architecture, research-recipient list, deletion semantics, or demonstrated anonymization process. [R02](#receipt-02)

The missing documentation is a gap. It is **not evidence of clandestine collection, misuse or an existing breach**.

But the sensitivity of gaze data is established by independent research. Studies have shown that eye-movement traces can carry identifying information under particular experimental conditions. A 2026 paper reports **96.61% identification accuracy in its own VR dataset and classifier**. That number does not describe LUCHS and must not be used as if it did. It does explain why changing a name to a code is not, by itself, a proof of anonymity. [R29](#receipt-29) [R30](#receipt-30)

For a device meant to spend the day near someone's face, the relevant questions are operational: Does it retain eye images or only derived features? On whose hardware does inference occur? Are uploads encrypted? Who can access historical records? What happens after consent is withdrawn? Can an employer, insurer, software vendor or research institution obtain derived cognitive scores? How is the research dataset tested against re-identification?

Under the EU's data-protection framework, some health and identifying biometric uses can implicate special-category controls. The exact legal analysis depends on the actual data and purpose. [R31](#receipt-31)

Privacy is not a sentence in a hero banner. It is a tested set of boundaries between people, machines and permissions.

::: {.finding}
**WATCH VERDICT / PRIVACY**  
A no-outward-camera product can still process highly sensitive inward-looking data. The company should publish the production architecture and verification record before users are asked to trust its most intimate measurements.
:::

# 07 · Wellness Is Not a Medical Diagnosis

::: {.eyebrow}
CLAIM SCOPE / CLINICAL EVIDENCE / INTENDED USE
:::

The consumer proposition is not merely that eyes are interesting. The LUCHS site speaks about attention, fatigue, cognitive performance and the possibility that shared longitudinal data could help researchers learn to anticipate cognitive decline. [R01](#receipt-01)

The word **could** matters. So does the boundary between a self-observation product and a medical screening instrument.

To say *your pattern changed* is one kind of inference. To say *your cognitive function deteriorated* is stronger. To say *this predicts a future neurodegenerative condition* is stronger again. Each move changes the kind of ground truth, prospective study, error analysis and, potentially, regulatory controls required.

In the public documents reviewed, no LUCHS-specific FDA clearance or European medical-device certification was verified. Nor was final production labeling establishing exactly which health claims the company intends to make. That is not a conclusive statement of nonexistence. It is a statement about the inspected record. The FDA's January 2026 General Wellness guidance also distinguishes particular low-risk wellness uses from disease-focused medical uses. [R32](#receipt-32)

The important regulatory question is not whether the company is enthusiastic about health. It is **what the device claims to determine for a particular person, and how that determination is validated**.

The product can be useful without being a diagnostic instrument. But if it crosses that line in marketing or use, the line should be described, not smudged.

::: {.finding}
**DISPOSITION**  
The advertised research ambition is not presently a substantiated clinical prediction claim. The eventual intended use, evidence standard and authorization path require documentation.
:::

# 08 · What Changes the Verdict

::: {.eyebrow}
ACTIONABLE DOCUMENT REQUEST / DECISION GATES
:::

A competent investigation should be able to tell a company exactly what evidence would change its conclusion. Otherwise it is just suspicion wearing a lab coat.

**First, a production device.** Show the actual camera and illumination architecture, sampling rate, calibration scheme, missing-data rules, battery and thermal profile, prescription compatibility, safety testing, and the way it behaves under sunlight, reflection, motion and prolonged wear.

**Second, the full model specifications.** For each reported index, publish a measurement definition, scale, reference standard, training population, validation sample, reported uncertainty and conditions of failure. Separate the three measures a distributor said were available in 2026 from additional fatigue or composite functions that may still be in development. [R13](#receipt-13)

**Third, prospective external validation.** Use independent cohorts and prespecified outcomes. Hold out people, not merely recordings from the same person. Report performance under demographic and ophthalmic variation. Compare against ordinary confounders: light, sleep, medication effects, age, visual impairment and task changes. Publish failures.

**Fourth, medical evidence appropriate to any medical claim.** If the company claims disease prediction, provide actual clinical endpoints, follow-up, sensitivity, specificity, calibration and useful thresholds. If it does not, say plainly that this is a wellness or research product and state the limits.

**Fifth, a data-governance map.** Show where raw eye imagery goes, where derived data resides, who holds the keys, which processors can access it, how consent is separated between operating the glasses and donating research data, and how withdrawal propagates to old records.

**Sixth, clean provenance.** Supply the current certified Austrian register extract, external study protocols, methods and software versions, complete available whitepapers, conflicts of interest and funding disclosures. A corporate title discrepancy is a records question. Resolve it with records.

Those six requests are not an accusation. They are a reproducible route from an interesting company with some real science to a justified consumer claim.

::: {.finding}
**GATE CONDITIONS**  
BUILD AROUND THE SCIENCE: yes, as research.  
TREAT LUCHS AS CLINICALLY VALIDATED: not yet supported.  
ACCUSE THE COMPANY OF WRONGDOING: not supported by the reviewed evidence.  
REQUEST THE PRODUCTION RECEIPTS: justified.
:::

# Closing Watch · The Measured Thing

::: {.eyebrow}
FINAL HANDOFF / 28 OCT 2026
:::

SOMA Reality is not a cardboard front holding a set of glamorous frames. There is real talent, a recognizable funding trail, laboratory work, a published application and an academic record that includes findings worth taking seriously. That's the good news, and it is good news.

The hard part begins where the photograph ends.

A pupil changes. The camera notices. The software assigns meaning. The company promises you can learn something important about your cognitive health. Each arrow in that sequence must survive independent testing. A beautiful consumer product cannot outsource that responsibility to an older paper about a controlled task.

I am not recommending dismissal. I am not signing off on the claims, either. The file remains open at exactly the points where evidence can settle it: the final hardware, the independent validation and the privacy architecture.

A sensor can tell you what it observed. It is our job to know when somebody has mistaken that observation for a verdict.

::: {.final-line}
